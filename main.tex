\begin{abstract}
Reliable multi-UAV wildfire response requires closed-loop mission coordination that
converts uncertain aerial observations into executable fleet plans and
preserves mission continuity under runtime perturbations.
Existing UAV studies address these capabilities using different task inputs,
state representations, action spaces, resource assumptions, and evaluation
protocols, limiting direct assessment of initial mission planning and runtime plan repair
under common operating conditions. We therefore develop CLoMC-UWR, an LLM-based closed-loop mission
coordination system for multi-UAV wildfire response. CLoMC-UWR transforms aerial
observations into initial mission plans through Vision Analysis and Mission Planning,
audits candidate plans through Validation, retrieves relevant prior mission records
through Memory to inform subsequent planning, and invokes Runtime Replanning
after runtime perturbations. A
real-UAV-informed AirSim suite evaluates it across four difficulty levels with
resource constraints, scheduling constraints, and runtime perturbations. Across
four downstream foundation-model configurations, CLoMC-UWR outperforms an
Optimization-Based Planner in task completion and execution quality under hard
and expert conditions. Controlled ablations identify the condition-dependent
roles of Validation, Memory, and Runtime Replanning. On simple and normal episodes, a
fine-tuned Qwen3-8B planner retains 98.24\% of the Gemini~3.1~Pro teacher
planner's average score while reducing mean planning time from 37.50 to
14.01~s and mean planning-token usage from 13,353 to 3,593. These results
demonstrate the effectiveness of CLoMC-UWR for adaptive multi-UAV wildfire
response.
\end{abstract}
