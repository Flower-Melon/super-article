\begin{abstract}
Wildfire rescue is time-critical, yet affected areas are often hazardous and
difficult to access. Multi-UAV systems can provide rapid aerial observation
and intervention, but their effective deployment requires dynamic mission
coordination as the fire situation, UAV operational states, and mission
constraints evolve.
Existing UAV studies address these capabilities under different task
definitions and evaluation protocols, leaving unanswered whether a single
coordination system can generate a feasible initial multi-UAV mission plan and repair
it after runtime perturbations within the same operating conditions. We
therefore develop CLoMC-UWR (Closed-Loop Mission Coordination System for UAV
Wildfire Rescue), an LLM-based closed-loop mission
coordination system for multi-UAV wildfire rescue. CLoMC-UWR transforms aerial
observations into an initial mission plan through Vision Analysis and Mission Planning,
audits the candidate plan through Validation, retrieves relevant prior mission records
through Memory to inform subsequent planning, and invokes Runtime Replanning
after runtime perturbations. A
real-UAV-informed AirSim suite evaluates it across four difficulty levels with
resource constraints, scheduling constraints, and runtime perturbations. Across
four downstream foundation-model configurations, CLoMC-UWR outperforms an
Optimization-Based Planner in task completion and execution quality under hard
and expert conditions. Controlled ablations identify the condition-dependent
roles of Validation, Memory, and Runtime Replanning. On simple and normal episodes, a
fine-tuned Qwen3-8B planner retains over 98\% of the Gemini~3.1~Pro teacher
planner's average score while reducing mean planning time by approximately
63\% and mean planning token usage by approximately 73\%. These results
demonstrate the effectiveness of CLoMC-UWR for adaptive multi-UAV wildfire
rescue.
\end{abstract}
